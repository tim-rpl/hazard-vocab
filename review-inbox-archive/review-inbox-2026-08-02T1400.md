### A32.1 — settled. Survives, exactly as filed.

`Constraint` is `sig Constraint {}` — an opaque atom with no fields.
`Profile` carries `adds : set Constraint` and `drops : set Constraint`.
`composes` is union of `adds` and union of `drops`. **There is no
sequence, no ordering relation, and no way to express precedence
anywhere in the file.** Reordering is not merely undetectable, it is
inexpressible, so `check_restrictionSound` and
`check_compositionPreservesSoundness` are silent on it. A32.1 moves from
*unverified by O* to **survived**.

### F10 — the Alloy model proves a set-algebra tautology, and the `Part` half of it is inert

This is what §4's three questions produce when applied to
`check_restrictionSound`, and it is why I declined to record the UNSAT
as T2 evidence when I could not read the file. The reason is stronger
than the one I gave.

**1. What does it state?** For every `p` with no drops and every `i`:
if `(Base.constraints + p.adds) ⊆ i.satisfies` then
`Base.constraints ⊆ i.satisfies`.

**2. Could it hold vacuously?** Not by empty quantification — the
hypotheses are satisfiable. It holds by **pure set algebra**:
`A ∪ B ⊆ S ⟹ A ⊆ S`. There is no profile, no LinkML, no SHACL, no
cardinality and no enum in that proposition.

I ran two mutations to make that an experiment rather than a reading:

| Mutation | Result |
|---|---|
| **M1** — delete `sig Part` and `fact partsAcyclic` outright | **identical output**: UNSAT / SAT / UNSAT |
| **M2** — every `check ... for 6` raised to `for 20` | **identical output**: UNSAT / SAT / UNSAT |

M1: the `Part` signature, the `imports` relation and the acyclicity fact
are referenced by **no assertion in the file**. They can be deleted with
no effect. The header says *"Structural claims about profiles and parts.
See claims.md T2, C1, C2."* Parts are asserted about by nothing, and
**C1 and C2 are cited in the header and tested by nothing.**

M2: the scope-6 caveat that `scripts/alloy.sh` prints, and that §4
requires me to record, is beside the point for these two assertions.
They are not scope-limited evidence. They are scope-free and true.

**3. Does it state what T2 needs?** No — and this is the part that
matters. T2's own falsifier names three relaxations: *"a profile that
widens a cardinality, removes a required slot, or extends an enum's
permissible values."* In this model all three are representable only as
`drops`, because `effective` is **defined** as
`(Base.constraints - p.drops) + p.adds` — so `adds` can only ever shrink
the set of valid instances.

The third one breaks that. In LinkML, extending an enum's permissible
values is an **addition** that **relaxes**: `sh:in` gains a member and
more instances validate. T2 counts that as a relaxation. The model has
no way to represent it as anything but an `add`, and no way to detect it
being classified as one.

So `check_restrictionSound` establishes: *if relaxations are correctly
classified as drops, then profiles that do not drop are sound.* That is
T2's conclusion assumed into the encoding. It is not vacuous — it is
circular, which is the harder case to see, and it would stay UNSAT even
in a world where LinkML profiles relaxed by addition as a matter of
routine.

**T2 gets no evidence from this artifact.** Recorded on the claim.

### F12 — the jurisdiction rule inspects names only, and the project's own convention puts scheme identity somewhere else

The docstring states one limitation honestly: *"A jurisdiction-specific
scheme with a non-acronym name passes."* Two gaps go past that.

**Uninspected fields, and they are the designated ones.** CLAUDE.md
Conventions: *"Code lists are SKOS concept schemes... LinkML enums
reference them via `PermissibleValue.meaning`."* Neither `meaning` nor
`slot_uri`, `class_uri` or the `prefixes:` block is inspected. This file
passes clean:

```yaml
prefixes:
  airnow: https://airnow.gov/scheme/
slots:
  siteIdentifier:
    slot_uri: airnow:AQSID
enums:
  IdentifierSchemeEnum:
    permissible_values:
      siteIdentifier:
        meaning: airnow:AQSID
```

`exit=0`, `jurisdiction ok`. Every name is generic; the jurisdiction is
carried entirely in the URIs. Once the project follows its own
conventions, names are the place jurisdiction content is *least* likely
to appear — and a declared prefix pointing at an agency domain is the
cheapest signal available and the one nothing looks at.

**The acronym bound is 8 characters and undocumented.**
`ACRONYM = ^[A-Z][A-Z0-9]{1,7}$`. `CALFIREINC` and `AQSIDLONGX` (10
characters each) pass. Those are acronyms, so the stated limitation
does not cover them.

Neither gap is a block. F12 is the reason C18 stays `falsified` on
recall rather than moving to `tested`.
